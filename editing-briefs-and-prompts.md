# 32. COPY-READY CINEMATIC AND FUNNY EDITING BRIEF

The following prompt is designed to be given to a human editor or an AI editing tool. Fill in the bracketed fields. If a field is unknown, state an assumption rather than inventing facts.

```text
PROJECT OBJECTIVE

Turn the supplied raw footage into a polished long-form YouTube video with a target runtime of [TARGET RUNTIME, e.g. 8–12 minutes], within the requested overall range of 5–20 minutes.

The finished video should feel cinematic, entertaining, and funny without becoming cluttered or overloaded with effects.

Content type: [GAMING / GAMEPLAY / VLOG / SHORT FILM / CHALLENGE / DOCUMENTARY / REACTION / OTHER]
Primary audience: English-speaking viewers in the United States, while keeping the story and humor understandable to international viewers.
Spoken language: [LANGUAGE]
Voice-over/narration language: [LANGUAGE / NONE]
Subtitle language: [LANGUAGE / BILINGUAL / NONE]
Subtitle format: [FULL BURNED-IN / SEPARATE SRT / SELECTIVE EMPHASIS TEXT / OTHER]
Humor style: [SITUATIONAL / DIALOGUE / REACTIONS / MEMES / ZOOMS / SOUND EFFECTS / DRY HUMOR / MIX]
Reference videos: [LINKS]
Visual references: [LINKS OR DESCRIPTION]
Music direction: [DESCRIPTION / NO MUSIC]
Target runtime: [EXACT TARGET]

NARRATIVE AND OPENING

Build a coherent story from the footage. Do not simply assemble a list of effects or a sequence of unrelated clips.

Use a compelling moment, funny situation, reaction, visual event, or story question in approximately the first 5–15 seconds. Do not reveal the complete result if doing so would remove the reason to keep watching. Do not create a misleading hook.

Explain the situation and the video's objective quickly. Build a clear progression with meaningful events, challenges, discoveries, reactions, complications, or twists. Lead into a satisfying climax or main payoff, then provide a clear ending.

Avoid a long logo animation or a generic intro before the actual content begins.

RHYTHM AND FOOTAGE SELECTION

Remove or shorten repetition, uneventful waiting, unnecessary loading screens, technical delays, redundant dialogue, and footage that does not contribute to the story, comedy, or viewer understanding.

Keep all context required to understand what happened and why the outcome matters.

Do not remove every pause or silence. Preserve pauses that create suspense, comedic timing, anticipation, emotional weight, or a genuine reaction.

Vary the pacing naturally. Use fast cuts for energy or surprise and longer shots when a scene, reaction, joke, or suspenseful moment needs room to work. Do not force a fixed cut interval or add visual changes on a timer.

HUMOR AND EDITING EFFECTS

Build humor first from the situation, dialogue, performance, timing, and genuine reactions.

Use punch-in zooms, freeze frames, short slow motion, captions, memes, sound effects, replays, or other visual jokes only when they make the moment funnier or clearer.

Do not overuse the same joke, zoom, sound effect, meme, or transition. Avoid clutter and random effects.

When adapting humor into English, preserve the intention and timing of the joke. Use natural American English. Do not use literal translations or forced American slang.

IMAGE AND COLOR

First correct exposure, white balance, and shot-to-shot consistency. Then apply a cohesive cinematic look that fits the content.

Preserve useful shadow and highlight detail. Keep skin tones natural when people are visible. Use noise reduction, stabilization, and sharpening only when required and without creating an artificial look.

Do not add letterbox bars, dramatic transitions, heavy lens effects, or slow motion without a clear creative reason. Cinematic quality should come from storytelling, shot choice, rhythm, sound, and consistent color—not decoration alone.

AUDIO AND MUSIC

Dialogue clarity is the highest audio priority. Keep dialogue levels consistent and reduce distracting noise when possible. Lower or automate background music under speech. Keep effects from masking dialogue.

Match music and sound design to the story, comedic beats, tension, and climax. Do not add music to every moment.

Use only music and sound effects with licenses that permit the intended YouTube use. Provide the source and license information for third-party audio assets.

Clipped or badly damaged audio may not be fully repairable. Improve it as much as practical, but do not promise perfect restoration.

For a stereo web-video mix, approximately -14 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP maximum true peak may be used as starting references, not as mandatory YouTube requirements. Preserve appropriate dynamics and prioritize actual listening quality.

SUBTITLES AND ON-SCREEN TEXT

Create accurate, readable subtitles that are synchronized with the final edit. Prefer no more than two lines at a time. Keep subtitles away from faces, key visual information, and important gameplay or interface elements.

Deliver a complete SRT file when requested. Use selective on-screen emphasis captions only for important phrases, reactions, or jokes unless full burned-in captions are specifically requested.

Do not invent dialogue or timestamps. Verify the subtitle file against the final exported video.

ENDING AND DELIVERY

Give the story a satisfying result or ending. Add a short, natural call to action only when it fits. Leave suitable visual and timing space for a YouTube end screen when appropriate.

Export the long-form master in 16:9 at [1080p / 4K / SOURCE-MATCHED RESOLUTION] and [SOURCE-MATCHED FRAME RATE / SPECIFIED FRAME RATE]. Use settings appropriate to the source footage. Do not upscale without a reason.

Create vertical Shorts or other short clips only if they are explicitly requested. They must be separate deliverables and must not replace or shorten the long-form master.

WORKFLOW AND QUALITY CONTROL

Before final editing, prepare a concise proposed structure, list the selected key moments, identify sections to remove or shorten, and explain the intended story and humor style.

If the source files are unavailable or cannot be inspected, do not guess their contents or timecodes. Clearly state what cannot be verified.

Before delivery, check audio/video synchronization, dialogue clarity, subtitle spelling and timing, unwanted black frames, abrupt music cuts, missing footage, export resolution, frame rate, aspect ratio, and successful playback.

Do not claim that an MP4, SRT, or editable project file has been exported unless the file was actually created and verified.
```

# 43. APPENDIX B — COMPLETE EDITING PROMPTS AND DELIVERABLE CONTRACTS

Use the template that matches the user's actual request. Fill only the relevant placeholders. Do not produce separate deliverables unless requested.

## B1. Universal Long-Form Editing Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a senior YouTube editor, story editor, comedy editor, sound designer, colorist, and post-production supervisor.

OBJECTIVE
Turn the supplied raw footage into a complete, polished YouTube long-form video. The required deliverable is [ACTUAL RENDERED MP4 / EDITING PLAN / EDITORIAL REVIEW]. Do not substitute a plan for a rendered video when actual editing and export are supported and requested.

PROJECT CONTEXT
Content type: [GAMEPLAY / GAMING CHALLENGE / VLOG / SHORT FILM / DOCUMENTARY / REACTION / OTHER]
Target runtime: [EXACT TARGET; e.g. 8–12 minutes]
Primary audience: [AUDIENCE AND COUNTRY]
Spoken language: [LANGUAGE]
Narration language: [LANGUAGE / NONE]
Subtitle language: [LANGUAGE / BILINGUAL / NONE]
Subtitle delivery: [SRT / BURNED-IN / BOTH / NONE]
Humor style: [SITUATIONAL / DIALOGUE / REACTION / DRY / ABSURD / EDITORIAL / MIX]
Visual style: [REFERENCE OR DESCRIPTION]
Cinematic references: [LINKS OR NONE]
Music direction: [STYLE / LICENSED MUSIC / ORIGINAL SCORE / NO MUSIC]
Output aspect ratio: [16:9 / OTHER]
Resolution: [SOURCE-MATCHED / 1080p / 4K]
Frame rate: [SOURCE-MATCHED / SPECIFIED]
Required files: [MP4 / SRT / PROJECT FILE / OTHER]

CORE EDITORIAL RULE
Build a coherent story, not a montage of effects. The video must have a strong opening, quick context, meaningful progression, escalation, a satisfying payoff, and a clean ending.

OPENING
Use a genuine strong moment or clear story question in approximately the first 5–15 seconds. Confirm the title/thumbnail promise quickly. Do not invent footage, misrepresent an event, or reveal the complete payoff when that would weaken the story.

FOOTAGE SELECTION
Review the available media. Remove or shorten repetition, empty waiting, irrelevant footage, technical delays, and redundant dialogue. Preserve context, meaningful reactions, suspense, comedic pauses, and moments required to understand the outcome.

CINEMATIC TREATMENT
Create the cinematic feel through shot selection, composition, pacing, continuity, color consistency, sound perspective, and narrative progression. Do not rely on black bars, fake camera movement, heavy grain, lens flares, slow motion, or transition effects as decoration.

COMEDY
Build jokes from the actual situation, dialogue, performance, expectation, contrast, reactions, and timing. Use zooms, freeze frames, captions, memes, replays, and sound effects only when they strengthen a real joke. Preserve pauses when they improve comedic timing. Avoid repeated or random effects.

EDITING RHYTHM
Use both fast and long holds where appropriate. Do not cut at a fixed interval. Do not add a pattern interrupt on a timer. The pacing must follow the story, action, information density, and emotional state.

IMAGE AND COLOR
Inspect the source first. Correct exposure and white balance, match shots, preserve highlight and shadow detail, and apply a cohesive grade. Use denoising, stabilization, and sharpening only when required. Do not create artificial detail or change SDR/HDR tags incorrectly.

AUDIO
Dialogue is the priority. Synchronize recordings, reduce distracting noise conservatively, control tonal problems only when needed, balance music under speech, preserve dynamics, avoid clipping, and check phase/mono compatibility. Clipped audio may not be fully repairable. Do not promise perfect restoration.

SUBTITLES
Create an accurate, synchronized subtitle file in valid UTF-8 SRT when requested. Prefer concise lines and no more than two lines at once where practical. Keep captions clear of faces and essential UI. Verify all cues against the final export. Do not invent dialogue or timestamps.

RIGHTS
Use only media, music, fonts, and effects with permissions appropriate to the intended YouTube use. Record sources and license terms for third-party assets. Do not assume attribution alone grants permission.

WORKFLOW
1. Inspect and inventory the supplied media.
2. Prepare a concise story outline and selects log.
3. Build the assembly and rough cut.
4. Refine story, comedy, and pacing.
5. Finish audio, color, graphics, and captions.
6. Export the requested files.
7. Inspect the actual exports and report QC results.

SUCCESS CRITERIA
- The final video fulfills its actual title/thumbnail promise.
- The narrative is clear and complete.
- The target runtime is respected without padding.
- Dialogue is intelligible and audio is balanced.
- Visuals are consistent and free of avoidable defects.
- Subtitles are accurate and synchronized when requested.
- Export settings match the source and delivery requirements.
- All requested files exist and have been verified.

ACTION BOUNDARIES
Do not overwrite original files. Do not delete source media. Do not upload or publish publicly unless explicitly authorized. Do not purchase assets, subscribe to paid services, or use unlicensed material. Ask before changing the intended story, language, audience, or deliverables in a material way.

If the tool cannot actually edit or export, state the limitation and provide only the deliverable it can genuinely produce. Never claim an MP4 or SRT exists unless it has been created and verified.
```

## B2. Footage Analysis and Edit-Decision Prompt

```text
Analyze the supplied footage for a YouTube long-form edit. Do not edit or export in this task.

Return:
1. Media inventory and technical properties;
2. A chronological event log with exact source timecodes;
3. The strongest opening candidates;
4. The key story beats;
5. Funny, emotional, surprising, or visually strong moments;
6. Repetitive or low-value sections that may be trimmed;
7. Context that must be preserved;
8. A proposed beginning, middle, climax, and ending;
9. A target runtime estimate based on actual footage;
10. Missing media, sync problems, audio defects, and rights questions.

Use exact timecodes only after inspecting the source. Mark anything not verified as [UNVERIFIED]. Do not invent events, dialogue, or footage.
```

## B3. Autonomous AI-Editor Execution Prompt

```text
OBJECTIVE
Edit the supplied source footage and deliver the actual finished video file in the requested format.

STARTING STATE
The source files are in [UPLOAD / FOLDER / PROJECT]. The requested output is [FORMAT, RUNTIME, RESOLUTION, FRAME RATE].

TARGET STATE
A fully rendered and verified final video, plus [SRT / EDITABLE PROJECT / OTHER] if requested.

ALLOWED ACTIONS
- Inspect supplied media;
- Create a non-destructive edit project;
- Make in-scope cuts and adjustments;
- Create proxies or cache files where needed;
- Add licensed or user-supplied graphics, music, and sound effects;
- Export the requested final files;
- Verify the exported files.

FORBIDDEN ACTIONS
- Do not delete or overwrite original media;
- Do not publish, upload, or share the video externally without approval;
- Do not use paid stock, paid plugins, or external services without approval;
- Do not invent footage, dialogue, sources, licenses, or timecodes;
- Do not add unrequested Shorts or extra deliverables;
- Do not change the target audience, language, story, or core creative direction without approval.

STOP CONDITIONS
Pause and ask before destructive changes, external publication, purchases, paid assets, unlicensed materials, or material changes to the requested output. If export is impossible, stop and report the exact blocker; do not pretend that the video was rendered.

PROGRESS EVIDENCE
At the end, report the actual files created, file sizes, durations, export settings, checks performed, known limitations, and any incomplete tasks. Base every completion claim on the actual tool result or file inspection.
```

## B4. Editing Review / Revision Prompt

```text
Review the supplied edit against the approved brief. Do not make changes yet.

Evaluate:
- Story clarity;
- Opening and promise confirmation;
- Pacing and retention risks;
- Comedic timing;
- Reaction timing and pauses;
- Continuity;
- Visual hierarchy;
- Dialogue clarity;
- Music and SFX balance;
- Color consistency;
- Captions;
- Runtime;
- Ending and end-screen space;
- Copyright and licensing evidence;
- Export quality.

For each issue, provide:
1. Exact timeline timecode;
2. Observed issue;
3. Why it matters;
4. Recommended change;
5. Priority: Critical / High / Medium / Low;
6. Whether the fix can be made non-destructively.

Do not invent a problem or a timecode. If the media cannot be inspected, say so.
```

## B5. Separate Shorts Derivative Prompt

```text
Create [NUMBER] separate vertical Shorts from the approved long-form master. Do not modify or replace the long-form master.

For each Short, identify a self-contained moment with its own context, hook, development, and payoff. Avoid misleading cuts. Reframe for vertical 9:16 only when the important subject and context remain visible. Use captions that are readable on mobile. Deliver each Short as a separately named video file and include its source timecode range. Do not create Shorts if the source does not contain a complete, useful short-form moment.
```
