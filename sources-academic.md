# 38. ACADEMIC AND INDUSTRY REFERENCE LIBRARY

## 38.1 How to Use This Bibliography

This library preserves the academic references supplied in the user's research notes. Article titles and bibliographic details are retained in their publication language. **The metadata and every claimed finding have not all been independently checked against the publisher's full text in this document.** Before citing a paper for a specific statistic, verify the DOI, title, publication status, sample, method, table, and exact result on the publisher's page or in the paper itself. A DOI list is a research starting point, not proof that every paper supports every claim.

Repeated entries in the supplied bibliography have been consolidated by DOI or title. No unique source was intentionally removed. The supplied numbering skipped item 59; no missing item was invented.

## 39.1 Topic Selection and Viewer Preferences

- **Oh et al. (2025):** The supplied summary describes a choice experiment with 525 respondents in which participants preferred thumbnails containing text and shorter videos. Verify the study population, video context, definition of “shorter,” and measured outcome in the original paper. This is a preference within that experiment, not a universal growth rule.
- **Kim & Sutherland (2024):** Compares the importance and satisfaction of selection attributes in YouTube tourism content. Use it to study audience preferences in tourism, not to generalize directly to all niches.
- **Malik et al. (2024):** Examines YouTube's changing role in cultural-market gatekeeping. Keep findings about popularity, fandom, and gatekeeping within their study context.
- **Jing et al. (2025):** Examines familiarity and novelty in online fanfiction. It may inform hypotheses about familiarity and novelty, but it does not establish an ideal mix for YouTube videos.
- **Bravin et al. (2026):** Studies trend-following, atypicality, and social-media engagement. Use it to formulate hypotheses about differentiation versus imitation; do not treat it as a universal YouTube rule.

## 39.2 Titles, Thumbnails, and Curiosity

- **Quéré & Matias (2025):** The supplied summary describes extensive field experiments on headline concreteness and curiosity gaps. It reports that a moderate amount of information may outperform complete ambiguity or complete disclosure in that setting. Verify the number of experiments, platform context, audience, and effect size in the paper.
- **Qiu & Golman (2024):** Studies curiosity in news consumption. It can inform question framing and curiosity, but news consumption is not identical to fictional video or entertainment.
- **Cui et al. (2024):** Analyzes sentiment in titles and thumbnails in relation to video views. Predictive or observational relationships are not proof of causal effects.
- **Milev & Tabov (2025):** Compares headlines and thumbnails in Bulgarian digital media. Use it to examine emotional context in images while respecting its geographic and media context.
- **Gherheș et al. (2024):** Compares ChatGPT-generated and human-authored online media headlines. Online news headlines are not equivalent to YouTube video titles.

## 39.3 Sharing and U.S. Audience Motivation

- **Ihm & Kim (2024):** Examines personal relevance, identity, and recipient relationships in news sharing. Transfer these findings to fictional video only as hypotheses.
- **Dinh & Lee (2025):** Examines social and personal motivations and online brand-related activities. Check the study's exact constructs before applying it to YouTube film sharing.
- **Yunze et al.
