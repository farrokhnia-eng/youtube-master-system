# 37. OFFICIAL YOUTUBE AND PLATFORM REFERENCES

**Reference policy:** Official help and policy pages are the primary authority for current YouTube rules and product features. Help pages can change without changing their URLs. Verify them again before making a time-sensitive publishing, legal, or monetization decision.

   Primary help and product documentation.
2. **YouTube Help Community and Announcements** — https://support.google.com/youtube/community  
   Community support and product-related announcements. Distinguish official staff posts from user discussions.

## G3. Audio Measurement References

4. **ITU-R BS.1770-5** — measurement algorithms for audio programme loudness and true-peak level:  
   https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770/en

5. **EBU R 128** — broadcast loudness normalization recommendation, including a -23 LUFS programme loudness target for its broadcast context. Do not present that target as a YouTube requirement:  
   https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128

## G4. Reference-Use Rules

- YouTube Help is the authority for current YouTube upload recommendations, caption support, and platform features.
- ITU-R BS.1770 defines measurement methods; it does not itself prescribe a YouTube loudness target.
- EBU R 128 is a broadcast recommendation, not a universal web-video requirement.
- The approximately -14 LUFS integrated / -1 dBTP suggestion in the supplied editing brief remains a possible creator-side starting point, not an official YouTube specification.
- Use the source-matched frame rate and the current YouTube encoding table rather than hardcoding one export profile for every project.
- Do not promise that a particular bitrate, loudness target, or codec will prevent all platform re-encoding artifacts.

## G5. Editing Software Documentation

When a task depends on a specific editor, consult the current official documentation for that product and version. Common environments include Adobe Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, CapCut, and command-line media tools such as FFmpeg. Do not assume feature parity across products. Do not invent menu names or exact click paths when the version is unknown.
