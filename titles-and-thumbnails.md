# 10. TITLE ENGINEERING

A title should make the subject clear, establish a specific promise, create appropriate curiosity, and complement the thumbnail.

## 10.1 Title Approaches

1. Search-focused;
2. Curiosity-focused;
3. Story-based;
4. Emotional;
5. Educational;
6. Documentary;
7. Contrarian;
8. Question-based;
9. Challenge-based;
10. Outcome-based;
11. Mystery-based;
12. Comparison-based.

## 10.2 Writing Rules

- Place the main keyword near the beginning when that reads naturally.
- Use numbers only when they are accurate and useful.
- Use curiosity words when they fit the actual content.
- Do not invent urgency.
- Use parentheses or brackets only when they add clarity.
- Avoid repeating the thumbnail text word for word.
- Never make a misleading promise.

## 10.3 Title Length and Keyword Claims

Preserve the six-to-seven-word title guideline as a testable hypothesis, not a universal rule. A longer or shorter title may be better when it communicates the promise more clearly.

Keyword placement near the beginning is a clarity and relevance recommendation, not a universal algorithm requirement.

## 10.4 Translation Clarity Test

For bilingual content, a machine-translation check may help reveal whether a title communicates its basic meaning. It is not an official YouTube ranking signal and does not replace review by a fluent speaker.

## 10.5 Alternative Titles and CTR

Prepare three to five alternatives when useful. Review a title change around days three to seven only when enough impressions and comparable data exist. Treat CTR below 5% as a diagnostic prompt, not a universal threshold. Compare by traffic source, topic, audience, channel history, and sample size.

## 10.6 Title Deliverable

For a full title task, generate twelve distinct options across several approaches, shortlist the three strongest, and provide three to five alternatives. Explain the promise, intended viewer, thumbnail relationship, and risk of misunderstanding.

# 11. THUMBNAIL ENGINEERING

A thumbnail should communicate a clear visual idea quickly and at small mobile size.

## 11.1 Design Variables

- Visual hierarchy;
- Main subject;
- Facial expression;
- Eye direction;
- Eye contact;
- Subject separation;
- Outline;
- Contrast;
- Color harmony;
- Background depth;
- Composition;
- Lighting;
- Typography;
- Curiosity;
- Mobile readability;
- Emotional impact;
- Relationship to the title.

## 11.2 Core Principles

1. Establish one dominant visual message.
2. Use one main focal point unless the concept requires more.
3. Keep the background from competing with the subject.
4. Separate the subject clearly from the background.
5. Keep text short and readable.
6. Use arrows, circles, and emojis only when they add information.
7. Use faces and expressions when relevant to the concept.
8. Keep the image truthful to the video.
9. Test readability at mobile size.
10. Match the actual promise of the video.

## 11.3 Three Distinct Concepts

For a complete thumbnail task, consider:

A. A proven visual convention from the niche, without copying a competitor.  
B. A distinctive creative concept.  
C. The channel's own visual signature.

## 11.4 Visual Experiments

Test when appropriate:

- One line versus two lines of text;
- Text versus no text;
- One person versus a group;
- Direct eye contact versus an off-camera gaze;
- Outline versus no outline;
- Deep versus tight background;
- Before/after composition;
- Bright contrasting colors;
- Large subject versus smaller subject.

Do not assume one variation is best for every channel.

## 11.5 Technical Format

Use a 16:9 canvas for standard long-form thumbnails. 1280 × 720 is a common working size, but check current YouTube requirements for recommended dimensions, file type, and limits before export.

## 11.6 A/B Testing

When available, use YouTube's native Test & Compare tool for eligible videos. It can compare up to three eligible variants. Use the result reported by the tool; do not judge the winner solely by raw CTR if YouTube reports watch-time share. Check current eligibility and format limitations before planning the test.

For channels without native testing access, a Community poll can provide qualitative feedback. It is not a substitute for an actual thumbnail experiment with viewers who encounter the video in its normal distribution context.

# 12. THUMBNAIL PSYCHOLOGY CLAIM REGISTER

Preserve the following user-supplied numeric claims in the research registry. Verify the original study, sample, control condition, metric, and context before using them.

| Claim | Figure supplied | Default status |
|---|---:|---|
| Rapid initial image processing | About 13 milliseconds | Requires primary-source verification and precise definition |
| Direct eye contact increases CTR | About 20% | Requires original study and sample |
| Single person with outline vs. group image | 37.4% more views | Requires original study |
| Same single-person comparison | 18.6% more likes | Requires original study |
| Two-line thumbnail text vs. one line | 44.2% more likes | Requires original study |
| More distant background | 53.7% more likes | Requires original study |
| More distant background | 36.5% more views | Requires original study |

Do not combine these percentages as if they came from one experiment. Do not turn them into mandatory design rules without verifying the actual sources.
