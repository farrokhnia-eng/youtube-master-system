# 14. SCRIPTWRITING AND STORYTELLING METHODS

First identify the format:

- Documentary;
- Educational;
- Entertainment;
- Explainer;
- Commentary;
- Tutorial;
- Review;
- Storytelling;
- Fiction;
- Cinematic film;
- Short-form;
- Interview.

Do not force the same structure onto every format.

## 14.1 Methods Supplied in the User's Research Notes

### 1. Ben Kitchen / George Blackman — Open Curiosity Loops

Open meaningful questions, sustain curiosity, and close each loop at the right time. Avoid opening too many questions without paying them off.

### 2. MrBeast Team — Sawtooth Escalation

Build rising stakes, meaningful changes, new events, and recurring re-engagement moments. Do not treat a 60–90-second re-hook interval as a universal rule or attribute it definitively to a creator team without a primary source.

### 3. Tim Schmoyer — Hook, Promise, Payoff

Establish a compelling opening, make a clear promise, and deliver the promised value.

### 4. Paddy Galloway — CCN

Plan for Core, Casual, and New audience segments rather than writing only for the most dedicated viewers.

These are creator-practice frameworks, not official YouTube systems or guaranteed scientific formulas. Verify attribution when a precise method is attributed to a named creator.

## 14.3 General Long-Form Structure

1. Opening hook;
2. Establish the promise;
3. Introduce the central question or problem;
4. Develop the story or explanation;
5. Present evidence, demonstrations, or examples;
6. Add escalation or a new insight;
7. Deliver the main payoff;
8. Provide a satisfying conclusion;
9. Add a natural call to action.

Avoid generic greetings, long introductions, repeated information, suspense without payoff, and unsupported claims.

# 15. HOOKS, RETENTION, AND RE-ENGAGEMENT

Keep the following timings as planning prompts or hypotheses, not mandatory rules:

| Timing | Suggested purpose |
|---|---|
| First 3 seconds | Glance test and immediate attention |
| First 7 seconds | Confirm that the video matches the click promise |
| First 10–15 seconds | Cold open and rapid entry into the subject |
| First 30 seconds | Establish value and create the next meaningful reason to watch |
| Every 60–90 seconds | Possible re-engagement beat, if the story needs one |

The user-supplied claim that 20–40% of viewers leave during the first 10 seconds must remain in the claim registry, but must not be treated as a universal benchmark without source and channel data.

Every segment should have a clear job:

- Introduce;
- Develop a question;
- Create conflict;
- Reveal new information;
- Change the situation;
- Prove a point;
- Surprise the viewer;
- Deliver a result.

Use retention graphs to examine dips, spikes, rewatch segments, and drop-offs. Every curiosity loop should eventually receive a meaningful answer or payoff.

# 16. CINEMATIC FILMS AND AI STORY DEVELOPMENT FOR U.S. AUDIENCES

For fictional AI films in the 5–25-minute range, especially for U.S. audiences, do not assume one winning genre or a guaranteed viral topic.

Analyze three distinct viewer behaviors:

1. Selection or click;
2. Continued viewing;
3. Actual sharing.

## 16.1 Story Development

- Premise;
- Logline;
- Theme;
- Genre;
- Target audience;
- Character design;
- Character motivation;
- Conflict;
- Stakes;
- Plot;
- Emotional arc;
- Mystery;
- Suspense;
- Twist;
- Ending.

## 16.2 Audience Fit

Ask:

- Is the premise understandable to the intended U.S. audience?
- Can viewers identify with the characters or their dilemma?
- Does the story connect to a recognizable human experience?
- Is there a meaningful reason to share the film?
- Is the language natural and culturally appropriate?
- Does the story avoid generic or overused tropes?

For American English content, write natural U.S. English. Do not use forced slang or literal translations of jokes. Keep the story understandable to international English-speaking viewers where possible.

## 16.3 Testable Sample Concepts

These are creative examples, not proven high-view topics:

| Direction | Premise | Opening question |
|---|---|---|
| Technology mystery | A doorbell camera shows a visitor who will arrive tomorrow | Why is the timestamp from tomorrow? |
| Family drama | A father receives a message from his child's future | What will saving the child cost him? |
| Workplace comedy | An employee must train an AI replacement for his own job | What will he refuse to teach it? |
| Ethical fantasy | Every time someone helps a stranger, one cherished memory disappears | Would you spend your last memory? |

For each concept, separately evaluate why someone might click, keep watching, and share it.
