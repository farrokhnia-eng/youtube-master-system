# 25. ANALYTICS AND PERFORMANCE DIAGNOSIS

## 25.1 Long-Form Metrics

- Impressions;
- Impressions click-through rate;
- Views;
- Watch time;
- Average view duration;
- Average percentage viewed;
- Audience retention;
- First-30-second retention;
- Traffic sources;
- Unique viewers;
- New viewers;
- Returning viewers;
- Subscribers gained;
- End-screen click rate;
- Likes;
- Dislikes;
- Comments;
- Shares.

## 25.2 Shorts Metrics

- Shown in feed;
- How many chose to view / stayed to watch, as currently labeled;
- Engaged views;
- Average view duration;
- Average percentage viewed;
- Retention;
- Shares;
- Subscribers gained;
- Traffic sources.

Metric names and definitions can change. Use the current YouTube Studio definitions when analyzing a report.

## 25.3 Diagnostic Hypotheses

**LOW IMPRESSIONS:** Investigate topic demand, audience fit, competition, content history, and distribution sources.

**LOW CTR:** Investigate title, thumbnail, traffic source, viewer expectations, and audience match.

**LOW RETENTION:** Investigate the opening, promise delivery, narrative, pacing, and production quality.

**HIGH CTR / LOW RETENTION:** Investigate a mismatch between the packaging promise and the actual video, or a weak opening.

**HIGH RETENTION / LOW REACH:** Investigate topic size, demand, audience expansion, competition, and distribution sources.

**VIEWS WITHOUT SUBSCRIBER CONVERSION:** Investigate channel positioning, content-to-brand fit, series structure, and reasons to return.

These are diagnostic hypotheses, not automatic conclusions. Compare videos with similar formats, lengths, topics, and traffic sources. Do not set one universal CTR target for every channel.

# 26. DIAGNOSING AND REVIVING UNDERPERFORMING VIDEOS

Use the term **stagnant performance** unless the data supports a more specific diagnosis. A video is not necessarily “dead” or “frozen” because its views slow down after 48 hours.

## 26.1 Initial Diagnosis

Check:

- Impressions;
- Traffic source;
- CTR by source;
- Retention;
- Title/thumbnail fit;
- Current topic demand;
- Audience match;
- Whether the video fulfills its promise.

## 26.2 Title and Thumbnail Changes

Preserve days three to seven after publishing as one possible review window, not a mandatory schedule. CTR below 5% is a prompt for investigation, not a universal threshold. Prepare three to five alternatives when useful.

If native A/B testing is available, use it. If it is not, label manual changes as hypotheses and compare results carefully. A change does not guarantee that YouTube will retest the video with a new audience.

## 26.3 Retention Drops and YouTube Editor

Review Key Moments for Audience Retention:

- Dips;
- Spikes;
- Rewatches;
- Drop-offs.

Consider removing a long greeting, redundant introduction, or unrelated section only when the edit improves the video without harming context, continuity, or the payoff. Check current Editor limitations and whether saved edits can be reverted before making major changes.

## 26.4 Related Traffic

Consider relevant:

- End screens;
- Cards;
- Description links;
- Pinned comments;
- Playlists;
- Community posts.

Send the video to viewers who are likely to value it. None of these tactics guarantees renewed distribution.

## 26.5 Evergreen Revival

Monitor evergreen videos for long-term Search and Suggested traffic. A video may regain attention months later, but this is not guaranteed.

# 30. EXPERIMENTATION AND A/B TESTING

Every experiment should include:

- Hypothesis;
- Audience;
- Variable;
- Control;
- Test variants;
- Primary metric;
- Secondary metric;
- Sample-size considerations;
- Observation period;
- Result;
- Decision.

Possible experiments:

- Title;
- Thumbnail;
- Title and thumbnail combination;
- Opening hook;
- Video length;
- Story structure;
- Music;
- Editing rhythm;
- CTA;
- Publishing time;
- Community post format.

Control the main variable when possible. Do not declare a winner from a small or inconclusive sample.

When available, use YouTube's native Test & Compare. The current documentation reviewed for this version says the tool can compare up to three title/thumbnail options and selects the option with the highest watch time. Current eligibility guidance says the feature is available on desktop in YouTube Studio, requires Advanced Features, and does not support Shorts, scheduled Live streams, Premiere videos before they become regular videos, private videos, Made for Kids videos, or videos designated for mature audiences. Recheck the live Help page before planning a test because eligibility and interface details may change.
