# 20. COMMUNITY POSTS, POLLS, AND PINNED COMMENTS

## 20.1 Community Tab

Use appropriate formats:

- Poll;
- Text post;
- Image post;
- Video post;
- Quiz;
- Announcement;
- Behind-the-scenes post;
- Content teaser;
- Question;
- Follow-up discussion.

Preserve these user-supplied claims as hypotheses requiring verification:

- Polls produce 5–10 times as much engagement as text posts;
- Three to five posts per week is an effective cadence;
- That cadence produces two to three times the comment rate on main videos.

Do not use these figures as universal rules without primary evidence. Set the posting cadence based on production capacity, audience response, and post quality. Avoid repetitive, low-value, or manipulative polls.

## 20.2 Pinned Comments

Create a pinned comment when it adds value. Main formats:

1. Open-ended question;
2. Useful resource;
3. Related playlist;
4. Next-video teaser.

Other useful types include clarification, additional information, source links, corrections, creator follow-up, and audience feedback.

A pinned comment should sound natural and match the video. Avoid meaningless engagement bait. Do not guarantee that pinning a comment improves ranking.

# 21. PUBLISHING AND PRE-RELEASE QUALITY CONTROL

## 21.1 Technical Checks

- Resolution;
- Frame rate;
- Aspect ratio;
- Audio quality;
- Encoding;
- Captions;
- HD/4K processing;
- Playback;
- Copyright checks.

## 21.2 Content Checks

- Title;
- Thumbnail;
- Description;
- Chapters;
- Tags;
- Playlist;
- End screen;
- Cards;
- Pinned comment;
- Audience setting;
- AI disclosure.

## 21.3 Private, Unlisted, and Scheduled Uploads

Use Private or Unlisted when you need to review quality or control access. Allow the required processing to finish. HD/4K processing can take hours depending on the file, resolution, frame rate, duration, and platform load.

The claims that a video must remain private for 24–48 hours or be uploaded six hours before the audience peak so the algorithm can analyze it are not established official requirements. Set the schedule based on actual processing and review needs.

## 21.4 Upload Defaults

Review available defaults for:

- Standard description;
- Channel links;
- Language;
- Category;
- License;
- Comment settings;
- Visibility;
- Caption settings;
- Relevant default tags, if useful.

# 22. PUBLISHING-TIME STRATEGY

Preserve three ways to investigate publishing time:

## Method 1 — YouTube Analytics

Review the Audience report, including `When your viewers are on YouTube`, when available. Use this report as audience-activity context. It reflects recent viewer activity and can help plan Premieres and Live streams.

## Method 2 — Controlled Time Tests

For a new channel, test different time windows using enough comparable uploads. Keep topic, format, audience, and other conditions as similar as practical. The suggested windows of 11:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. and 7:00–8:00 p.m. are only example test windows; local time must match the intended audience.

## Method 3 — Competitor Timing

Record when relevant competitors publish. This is contextual information, not proof of your own channel's best publishing time. Turning on competitors' notifications does not establish a universal “golden hour.”

## 22.1 Timing Claims to Preserve

Keep the following hypotheses in the claim registry:

- Publish one to two hours before the audience activity peak;
- Publish six hours before the audience activity peak;
- Test around ten comparable uploads;
- Record performance during the first four hours;
- Test 11:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. and 7:00–8:00 p.m. when appropriate for the target time zone.

Do not present any of these as official rules. YouTube's published guidance says that publish time is not known to affect long-term video performance, although audience activity can be useful for early viewing and for Live/Premiere planning.

# 23. FIRST-HOUR AND POST-PUBLISHING WORKFLOW

Preserve the idea of active creator availability during the first hour as a community-management practice, not an algorithm requirement.

Suggested actions:

1. Confirm that the video plays and the quality is correct.
2. Check the description, links, and settings.
3. Publish and pin the prepared comment.
4. Reply naturally to early comments.
5. Record errors, questions, and audience feedback.
6. Note initial analytics without drawing conclusions from a tiny sample.

When replying, respond to the viewer's actual point. Ask an open-ended follow-up only when it feels natural. Do not post repetitive questions simply to inflate comment counts.

# 27. PLAYLISTS, END SCREENS, CARDS, AND CONTINUED VIEWING

Use playlists to organize related content and help viewers find a logical next video.

When useful, include:

- A relevant playlist in the description;
- A complementary video or playlist in the end screen;
- Cards that lead to genuinely related content;
- A pinned comment with an additional resource or next step;
- A Community post that introduces relevant content.

Do not place a playlist link in every video by default. Relevance and viewer value come first. Do not guarantee increased session time or algorithmic rewards from playlist use.
