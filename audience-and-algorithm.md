# 8. AUDIENCE PSYCHOLOGY AND THE CCN FRAMEWORK

## 8.1 Audience Groups

**CORE:** Viewers with a clear interest in the channel's subject and style.  
**CASUAL:** Viewers who sometimes respond to the channel's topics.  
**NEW:** People encountering the channel or video for the first time.  
**RETURNING:** People who have watched the channel before and come back.

This is an editorial framework. It is not identical to YouTube's official New, Casual, and Regular Viewer reporting. Returning Viewers is a separate Analytics concept.

## 8.2 Psychological Factors

Evaluate relevant factors such as:

- Curiosity gap;
- Cognitive load;
- Emotional relevance;
- Personal relevance;
- Narrative transportation;
- Surprise;
- Novelty;
- Familiarity;
- Identification;
- Anticipation;
- Conflict;
- Suspense;
- Humor;
- Social identity;
- Information value;
- Entertainment value;
- Cognitive engagement;
- Emotional engagement.

For each concept, ask:

> Who would share this video with whom, and why?

Possible sharing motivations include entertainment, surprise, excitement, identification, personal relevance, discussion, usefulness, identity expression, and shared experience. These are possible motives, not guaranteed sharing triggers.

# 9. YOUTUBE RECOMMENDATIONS AND VIEWER SATISFACTION

Treat YouTube recommendations as a personalized system, not as a single fixed formula.

## 9.1 Signals and Contexts to Examine

- Watch history;
- Search history;
- Viewer preferences;
- Topic affinity;
- Similar viewer interests;
- Video selection and rejection;
- Watch time;
- Average view duration;
- Average percentage viewed;
- Impressions click-through rate;
- Likes and dislikes;
- Shares;
- Comments;
- Not Interested feedback;
- Don't Recommend Channel feedback;
- Satisfaction surveys;
- Traffic sources;
- Device and viewing context;
- Competition for viewer attention.

YouTube uses multiple signals to predict what each viewer may find valuable. Do not invent internal weights or a precise ranking formula.

## 9.2 Viewer Satisfaction

Consider video selection, viewing duration and percentage, repeat viewing, returning viewers, shares, positive and negative feedback, satisfaction surveys, and whether the video delivers its title/thumbnail promise.

Preserve the simplified model sometimes circulated in creator education:

`CTR × AVD × Satisfaction Factor`

This is **not** an official YouTube ranking formula.

Do not claim that only four- and five-star survey responses count as valued watch time, that three stars are neutral, or that a fixed rating threshold boosts distribution unless a primary official source explicitly supports the claim.

## 9.3 Session Continuation and Negative Feedback

Session continuation, repeat viewing, Not Interested, and Don't Recommend Channel are useful topics for understanding viewer behavior. Do not assume that every viewer who exits YouTube or does not watch another video creates a direct negative signal for the creator.

## 9.4 Multimodal AI Claims

Keep claims about Gemini, PLUM, semantic analysis of image/audio/text, and automated promise-content matching in the research registry. Verify the exact system, scope, and current status through reliable technical or official sources before presenting architectural details as facts.

## 9.5 New Viewer Attraction

Analyze new-audience growth using available metrics such as New Viewers, Unique Viewers, Returning Viewers, and New/Casual/Regular Viewer reports. Do not call “New Viewer Attraction” a separate official YouTube metric unless YouTube formally introduces it.
